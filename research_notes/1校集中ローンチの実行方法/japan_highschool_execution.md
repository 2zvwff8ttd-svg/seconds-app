# Japan high-school (高校生) launch: how social apps spread, school rules, laws for minors, safety and brand risk

Research date: 2026-11-21. Scope: Japan only. Tool limits: these hosts could not be reached from the session (DNS blocked): businessinsider.jp, webtan.impress.co.jp, markezine.jp, nishimura.com. Findings from those outlets come only from search-result snippets and are marked **[snippet-only]**. Total tool budget was about 16 calls, so many sub-questions remain gaps. Do not treat a gap as evidence that something does not exist.

## Q1. How did apps popular with Japanese high-schoolers spread, and how do 高校生 discover apps?

### Takeaway
Location-sharing "friend presence" apps reached a majority of high schoolers by 2023: whoo was the most-used "new SNS" (42.7%, Penmark, n≈130k) and 52% of high schoolers used some location-sharing app (LINE Research, n=1,037). The main stated reason was **"handy for meeting up" (待ち合わせに便利)**, which is the same job a "who's free" app does. I found **no primary survey that measures discovery channels** (friend word-of-mouth vs TikTok vs App Store), and no sourced case studies of whoo, NauNau, BeReal or Locket spreading through クラスLINE or 文化祭.

### Cited Findings
- Penmark 「高校生活実態調査」 (Aug 2023, about 130,000 high-school respondents): new SNS high schoolers had ever used were 1st **whoo 42.7%** and 2nd **NauNau 19.6%** — [Web担当者Forum 2023-09-11](https://webtan.impress.co.jp/n/2023/09/11/45585) **[snippet-only]**; also covered by [ASCII](https://weekly.ascii.jp/elem/000/004/158/4158544/) **[snippet-only]**
- LINE Research (national, 1,037 high schoolers; the publication date was not visible, likely 2023): 91% knew location-sharing apps/services (Google Maps, whoo, NauNau, etc.) and **52% "know and use"** one. whoo ranked 2nd after Google Maps. The top reason for using one was **"待ち合わせに便利だから"** — [MarkeZine](https://markezine.jp/article/detail/43591) **[snippet-only]**; [DIME](https://dime.jp/genre/1661299/) **[snippet-only]**
- whoo presents itself as an app for "becoming closer with friends." Location can be hidden with 「あいまい」 (fuzzy) and 「フリーズ」 (freeze) modes — [DIME](https://dime.jp/genre/1661299/) **[snippet-only]**
- Studyplus トレンド研究所 (July 2024, 1,401 high schoolers): high schoolers use **Instagram mainly to keep up with friends' news**, use X and TikTok for topic/interest information, and use LINE mostly for family contact. They also run multiple Instagram accounts (main, sub, 裏垢), and about half prune followers at least once a year — [Web担当者Forum/PR TIMES](https://webtan.impress.co.jp/r/prtimes/items/000000261.000047308) **[snippet-only]**
- ワカモノリサーチ (Apr–May 2025, n=242, small sample): **TikTok LIVE 50.8%** was the most-watched live platform among high schoolers — [Web担当者Forum 2025-05-22](https://webtan.impress.co.jp/n/2025/05/22/49277) **[snippet-only]**
- LINE Research (reported 2026-04-02): high schoolers' top news source is **TV**, while for male university students it is X. This suggests the information channels of high schoolers and university students differ — [Web担当者Forum](https://webtan.impress.co.jp/n/2026/04/02/52417) **[snippet-only, headline only]**

### Inferences
- The "meet-up utility" framing already won with high schoolers in whoo/NauNau. A "who's free" app with **no location sharing** can position itself as "待ち合わせ・予定合わせ without exposing your location," which answers the main parent and school worry about those apps (see Q4).
- Instagram is the "friends" network and TikTok is the "interest/discovery" network. A launch in one school therefore probably needs two paths: (a) friend-to-friend invite links sent via LINE or Instagram DM (the core loop), and (b) TikTok content for top-of-funnel awareness. This is an inference; no survey here measures app-discovery channels directly.
- Penmark itself (a timetable/school-life app with 13万 high-school respondents) is evidence that a school-life utility app can reach very large high-school penetration. Its growth mechanics were not researched here.

### Gaps
- No sourced data on **how high schoolers discover new apps** (friend recommendation vs TikTok vs App Store ranking). Expected sources (マイナビティーンズラボ, Z総研, ソニー生命 中高生が思い描く将来, LINEリサーチ) were not fetched within the budget.
- No sourced accounts of whoo, NauNau, BeReal, Locket or Zenly growth tactics in Japan (for example, whoo's TikTok creator use or school-by-school spread). No data on the role of クラスLINE, 部活LINE, 文化祭 (usually Sept–Oct), 体育祭, 修学旅行 or 春休み as adoption moments. These are commonly asserted, but I found no citation.
- No data on Studyplus, Clear or Penmark acquisition channels.

## Q2. School rules: smartphone use in Japanese high schools and the risk of school bans

### Takeaway
For high schools, MEXT leaves smartphone policy to each school (2020 policy). Most high schools allow phones on campus but restrict use (for example, during class or anywhere on campus). Promoting an app on school grounds therefore depends on the school's rules and should not be assumed to be allowed. I found **no sourced case of a school or board formally banning whoo/NauNau**. Public bodies issue general warnings about location-sharing apps (Q4).

### Cited Findings
- MEXT's 2009 national survey: roughly 94% of elementary and 99% of junior-high schools banned bringing phones in principle. Headlines reported about **20% of high schools** with an "in-principle ban." MEXT told high schools to restrict use on campus — [マイナビニュース 2009-02-03](https://news.mynavi.jp/article/20090203-mobileforbidding/) **[snippet-only; old data]**
- 2020 MEXT change: junior highs may allow phones under conditions, with no use on campus and phones collected or kept during school. **For high schools, each school should set its own policy** so that phones do not hinder education — [時事 (2020)](https://sp.m.jiji.com/english/show/6050) **[snippet-only]**; MEXT materials: [mext.go.jp 2020-05-28 PDF](https://www.mext.go.jp/content/20200528-mxt_jidou01-000007536_2.pdf) **[not fetched]**
- Saitama Prefecture survey (2018, full-time high schools): **42 schools** allowed phones but banned use anywhere on campus, **81** banned use only during class, and only **1** banned bringing phones in principle — [埼玉県 H30 PDF](https://www.pref.saitama.lg.jp/documents/24631/h30_sumahokekkasyousai.pdf) **[snippet-only]**
- Example of an individual school policy document on smartphone use: [岐阜県立坂下高校 sumaho.pdf](https://school.gifu-net.ed.jp/sakasita-hs/seitoshidou/sumaho.pdf) **[snippet-only, not read]**

### Inferences
- The typical high school allows phones but bans use during class, and a sizeable minority ban use anywhere on campus. A launch should therefore assume **in-school usage is restricted**. The natural usage windows are after school, on 部活 days off, on weekends and during long breaks (春休み, 夏休み). This fits a "who's free after school/weekend" product.
- Handing out flyers, putting up posters or doing a stage plug at a 文化祭 normally needs teacher or school approval. Doing it without approval risks the app being labeled "trouble" by 生徒指導. I found no source on specific rules for this.
- Being added to a school's or board's "注意が必要なアプリ" handout is the realistic ban mechanism, rather than a formal ban. Several prefectural and city handouts already name location-sharing apps (Q4).

### Gaps
- No **current national percentage** of high schools banning on-campus phone use. The MEXT 2020-era survey results were not retrieved, and I found no newer MEXT survey (2022–2026).
- No sourced instance of a Japanese high school formally banning whoo, NauNau, BeReal or similar. Not found does not mean none exist.
- I found no guidance on whether students may promote commercial apps at school.

## Q3. Laws and regulations for minors: filtering, App Store age ratings, APPI, stealth-marketing rules

### Takeaway
(1) The 青少年インターネット環境整備法 (2018 amendment) requires carriers to confirm whether a user is under 18 and to set up filtering on devices sold with contracts. On iPhone this filtering is typically Screen Time or carrier apps that restrict by **App Store age band**, so an app's rating decides whether it is blocked by default. (2) Apple overhauled ratings in 2025: 12+ and 17+ were replaced by **13+, 16+ and 18+**. Messaging/Chat, UGC and Shared Location by themselves map to **4+**, while a "Social Media" feed maps to 13+. A private 12-hour friends chat with no public feed may therefore rate low, but this must be confirmed in App Store Connect. (3) APPI: an amendment reportedly sets a **"under 16 → legal guardian consent/notice"** rule (Art. 40-2 per a law-firm newsletter). Its status and effective date need confirmation. (4) Since Oct 2023, ステマ規制 (景品表示法) requires PR disclosure whenever the company asks for or rewards posts, paid or not.

### Cited Findings
**Filtering (青少年インターネット環境整備法)**
- The 2018 amendment requires carriers and agents to confirm, at new or changed contracts, whether the contracting party or user is under 18, to explain filtering, and to **set up filtering on devices sold with the contract**. The scope was widened to smartphones and tablets — summarized from [総務省 PDF](https://www.soumu.go.jp:443/main_content/000528860.pdf) and [総務省 白書 R1](https://www.soumu.go.jp/johotsusintokei/whitepaper/ja/r01/text/nd242420.txt) **[snippet-only]**
- On iPhone, carriers name **Screen Time (iOS 12+)** as the filtering option, and docomo-style あんしんフィルター supports iOS 11+ — [Y!mobile filtering page](https://www.ymobile.jp/service/filtering/?wovn=en) **[snippet-only]**
- Parent guidance explains that iOS app restrictions are set by **age band (4+, 9+, …)**, not by school grade — [愛知県 PDF](https://www.pref.aichi.jp/uploaded/attachment/262185.pdf) **[snippet-only]**
- ITmedia (2025-06-05) warned that under the スマホ新法 (Mobile Software Competition Act, effective 2025-12-18), allowing third-party browser engines could weaken iPhone filtering. This is a **concern, not a confirmed effect** — [ITmedia](https://www.itmedia.co.jp/news/article/2506/05/1250605095/) **[snippet-only]**
- 内閣府/こども家庭庁 「令和6年度 青少年のインターネット利用環境実態調査」 surveys 5,000 youths aged 10–17, including filtering use — [e-Stat](https://www.e-stat.go.jp/stat-search/files?tstat=000001227643); summary [青森県 PDF](https://www.pref.aomori.lg.jp/soshiki/kodomo/kenmin/files/R7-RIN1-shiryou98-1_sankou_R6riyoukankyoutyousa_kekkagaiyou.pdf) **[snippet-only; high-school filtering % not retrieved]**
- Prefecture-level reference: Hiroshima's filtering use rate (all ages, not only high school) was **32.1% in R7 and 30.5% in R6** — [広島県 PDF](https://www.pref.hiroshima.lg.jp/uploaded/attachment/638668.pdf) **[snippet-only]**

**Apple age ratings and guidelines**
- Apple added **13+, 16+ and 18+** to 4+ and 9+. **12+ and 17+ were removed**, and existing apps were re-rated from earlier answers. The new questionnaire was required by **2026-01-31** for submitting updates — [Apple Developer News](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=ks775ehf); [MacRumors 2025-07-25](https://www.macrumors.com/2025/07/25/apple-overhauls-app-store-age-ratings/)
- The questionnaire capabilities are: Unrestricted Web Access, User-Generated Content, **Social Media**, Social Media Disabled for Users Under 13, **Messaging and Chat**, **Shared Location**, Advertising. In-App Controls are **Parental Controls** and **Age Assurance** (e.g., Declared Age Range API). In the global table, **Messaging and Chat, UGC, Shared Location and Advertising are listed at 4+**, while **Social Media (a feed that visibly spreads content to many users) is 13+** — [App Store Connect Help: Age ratings](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/reference/age-ratings) (fetched)
- The age-ratings page has **no Japan-specific rating section**. The regional sections cover Korea, Vietnam, Australia, Brazil and France — same source (fetched)
- Developers may set a **higher** rating than computed if their policy requires a higher minimum age — [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/apple-overhauls-app-store-age-ratings-with-new-categories) **[snippet-only]**
- App Review Guideline **1.2 (UGC)** requires: a method to filter objectionable material; a way to report content with timely responses; the ability to **block abusive users**; and published contact info. Apps used mainly for "random or anonymous chat … or **bullying**" may be removed without notice — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/) (fetched)
- **5.1.4(b)**: any app that collects or can share a minor's personal info (including "**the ability to chat**," photos, location) must have a privacy policy and comply with applicable children's privacy laws. Apps **outside the Kids Category must not imply children are the main audience** in the name, subtitle, icon, screenshots or description. **5.1.1(v)**: in-app account deletion is required. **2.3.6**: answer the age questions honestly so parental controls work — same source (fetched)

**APPI (個人情報保護法) and minors**
- A law-firm newsletter (Nishimura & Asahi, file dated 2026-07-28) says the amended APPI requires that, when a business handles personal information of **children under 16**, "本人" is read as "法定代理人" in key situations. In principle this means **notice to and consent from the legal guardian** (改正法40条の2) — [西村あさひ newsletter](https://www.nishimura.com/sites/default/files/newsletters/file/data_protection_260728_ja.pdf) **[snippet-only; status and effective date not verified]**
- PPC Guideline Q&A (revised 2025-07-01) index — [PPC APPI Q&A](https://www.ppc.go.jp/personalinfo/faq/APPI_QA/) **[fetched only as a snippet; the age-of-consent item was not located]**

**Stealth-marketing rules (景品表示法 ステマ規制, from 2023-10-01)**
- A designation under the 景品表示法 adds "a display where general consumers find it hard to tell it is the business's display" as an unfair representation. When a business instructs or requests third parties to post promotion, PR or reviews, **ad disclosure is mandatory**. The **advertiser is the regulated party**, and this can apply **even without money changing hands** (e.g., perks for posts, free product) — [ネットショップ担当者フォーラム](https://netshop.impress.co.jp/node/10843) **[snippet-only]**; [ecのミカタ](https://ecnomikata.com/column/42368/) **[snippet-only]**

### Inferences
- **Rating strategy:** a private friends-only 12-hour chat, invite links and no public feed should, per Apple's table, compute around **4+–13+**. If any feed, "who's out now" broadcast or public profile counts as **Social Media**, it becomes **13+**. Either rating passes the common under-18 Screen Time and carrier-filter settings, which parents usually set to the child's age (high schoolers are 15–18, so a 16+ limit is possible for first-years). Choosing **16+/18+ would cause default blocks for many 高1 students on filtered devices**. Verify the computed rating in App Store Connect before launch.
- Carrier filtering based on web categories may separately block the app's **web invite-link landing domain** (for example, "SNS/chat" categories). I did not verify this. Test invite links on a docomo/au/SoftBank filtered junior line.
- **APPI under-16:** high-school first-years can be 15. If the under-16 guardian-consent rule is in force (or coming), either set a **minimum age of 16** in the terms with age declaration (Declared Age Range API or a birthdate gate), or build a guardian-consent flow. Get legal confirmation of the amendment's status. Under the current law (before the amendment), the commonly cited practice is that minors of about 12–15 or younger need guardian consent; I could not cite this from the PPC.
- **Student ambassadors:** paying high schoolers, or giving them gifts, perks or "founder badges" for posts, is a ステマ risk unless each post says #PR. It also raises ethical, parental and school concerns. Rewards for **in-app invites** (not public posts) are less exposed to ステマ rules but still need care. Contracts with minors are voidable without parental consent (民法 general rule; not researched here).
- Because of 5.1.4, App Store copy and screenshots must not say "for 高校生/kids" in a way that implies children are the main audience. "For friends / 学生" is safer.

### Gaps
- Exact behavior of あんしんフィルター for iOS and of carrier defaults for under-18 lines (which age band is blocked) was not confirmed.
- The national **high-school filtering usage rate** (R6 内閣府/こども家庭庁 survey) was not retrieved.
- APPI amendment status (passed or effective date) and the PPC's current Q&A wording on children's consent age were not verified, because the Nishimura PDF host was blocked.
- Whether Japan's スマホ新法 changed iOS parental-control behavior in practice is unknown.
- I found no 消費者庁 guidance specific to minors acting as influencers.

## Q4. Safety expectations from parents and schools (いじめ, 仲間外れ) and how whoo/BeReal handled backlash

### Takeaway
Public warnings in Japan about friend-presence apps focus on **location exposure, being watched by acquaintances, and stalking or assault by "friends" met online**. They also mention **friend trouble**, but I found no sourced case linking the apps directly to 仲間外れ. A "who's free" app has a structurally similar risk: visible plans can show who was **not invited** (誘われなかった). Apple's 1.2 rule (report/block, and removal for bullying use) is the platform enforcement backstop.

### Cited Findings
- 北九州市 awareness material: location-sharing exposes where you are and how long you stay ("生活が丸見え"). If a friendship cools, the location may still be shared with someone you no longer trust or know — [北九州市 PDF](https://www.city.kitakyushu.lg.jp/files/000997737.pdf) **[snippet-only]**
- 埼玉県 net-trouble material (R4): cases where people connected as "friends" but never met in person **stalked** users or **came to their homes and assaulted** them — [埼玉県 R4 PDF](https://www.pref.saitama.lg.jp/documents/44152/r4_nettrouble05-2.pdf) **[snippet-only]**
- A Tochigi school handout warns that, depending on use, location apps **can cause trouble between friends** — [栃木県 黒磯南高校 handout](https://www.tochigi-edu.ed.jp/kuroisominami/nc3/wysiwyg/file/download/1/898) **[snippet-only]**
- Apple 1.2: apps used for bullying may be removed without notice. Report and block functions are mandatory — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)

### Inferences
- **Design to avoid making exclusion visible.** Do not show friends' plans to people who were not invited. Do not show "X and Y are hanging out" to third parties. Make availability ("暇") visible only to friends the user chooses, and let users hide from specific people quietly. The 12-hour chat expiry helps with evidence-free bullying worries but cuts both ways: teachers and parents may see ephemeral chat as hiding bullying. Prepare an answer (reporting keeps a copy for moderation).
- "No location sharing" is a strong **parent and school message**, given that every public warning found targets location exposure. It is worth putting in the App Store description and in any material for teachers or parents.
- Prepare a one-page 保護者・先生向け explanation (what's visible, blocking, reporting, contact, age policy). When a school's 生徒指導 asks, this is what keeps an app off "注意アプリ" handouts. This is inferred from how prefectures publish app-warning sheets.

### Gaps
- I found no sourced reporting on how **whoo, NauNau or BeReal responded** to criticism in Japan (feature changes, statements). The fuzzy and freeze modes exist, but I found no source saying they were a response to backlash.
- I found no NHK, 朝日 or 毎日 article in budget on school bans or bullying incidents tied to these apps.

## Q5. Ethical and brand risk of targeting high schoolers vs university students

### Takeaway
I found **no expert source** directly comparing high-school-first and university-first launches in Japan. The cited constraints together make high schoolers a higher-compliance segment than university students (mostly 18+): under-16 guardian-consent rules, carrier filtering for under-18s, school phone rules, and Apple's minors' data rules.

### Cited Findings
- Apple requires privacy-law compliance for any app that can collect or share minors' personal info, including chat and location, and forbids implying children are the main audience outside the Kids Category — [App Review Guidelines 5.1.4](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- Carriers must handle under-18 users with filtering by law — [総務省](https://www.soumu.go.jp:443/main_content/000528860.pdf) **[snippet-only]**
- Under-16 guardian consent and notice under the amended APPI — [西村あさひ](https://www.nishimura.com/sites/default/files/newsletters/file/data_protection_260728_ja.pdf) **[snippet-only]**
- High schoolers' information channels differ from university students' (TV vs X for news) — [Web担当者Forum 2026-04-02](https://webtan.impress.co.jp/n/2026/04/02/52417) **[snippet-only]**

### Inferences
- A pragmatic compromise is to launch in a high school whose students are mostly **16+ (高2–高3)**, set a 16+ minimum in the terms, and avoid paid student promotion. A lower-risk alternative is to start with university students and let it spread downward. This is inference only, not expert-sourced.
- The biggest brand risk is the app being named on a prefectural or school "注意が必要なアプリ" sheet. Visible-exclusion design (Q4) and no-location messaging are the main mitigations.

### Gaps
- I found no Japanese expert commentary (e.g., from 安心ネットづくり促進協議会, academics or NPOs) on startups targeting minors in particular.
