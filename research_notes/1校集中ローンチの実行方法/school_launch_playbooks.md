## Q1. For each app: the concrete steps (seed users, channel, launch timing, first 24–72 hours) and how it moved to the next school

### Takeaway
The best-documented playbook is Nikita Bier's (tbh in 2017, Gas in 2022). He picked **one** high school in Georgia because it had the earliest start date in the US. He saturated it with school-targeted ads plus a school-branded Instagram account that followed every student whose bio named the school, then accepted the follow-backs all at once so the whole school adopted together. He reports 40% of the school downloaded in 24 hours. He also stresses that this was a *testing* method for the first ~100 users, not how the app grew. After that, growth spread organically to neighbouring schools, released state by state with geofences. College apps (Facebook, Yik Yak, Fizz) instead used founder-run, on-campus channels: dorm/house networks, emails to Greek life and clubs, and donut/flyer giveaways. They expanded to nearby or peer campuses in clusters.

### Cited Findings

**tbh (Aug 2017) — Nikita Bier, Midnight Labs**
- School selection: "it immediately took off in the school that we seeded it into, in Georgia. We picked the one school that had the earliest start date in the United States because we needed to launch as soon as possible." So the launch was timed to the **first days of the school year**. — [Lenny's Podcast transcript (GitHub mirror)](https://github.com/ChatPRD/lennys-podcast-transcripts/blob/main/episodes/nikita-bier/transcript.md) [primary: transcript; founder claim]
- First 24 hours: "I think it spread to 40% of the school downloaded it in the first 24 hours and it rapidly spread to the neighboring schools… We will be number one in the United States in six days." — [transcript](https://github.com/ChatPRD/lennys-podcast-transcripts/blob/main/episodes/nikita-bier/transcript.md) [founder claim; secondary summaries vary between "24 hours" and "48 hours" — [DannyKPolitics Substack](https://dannykpolitics.substack.com/p/part-5-the-methods-of-nikita-bier)]
- Saturation channel (Bier's own description): "to be convinced to download an app, you need to see it… three times or so. So you basically need to saturate an area with every kind of marketing you can. So we ran ads targeted at a particular school… And we also followed people creating a dedicated Instagram account that went to that school, because we learned that high schoolers identify their school in their bio, so it says RHS on their bio… that was how we tried to get the entire school to adopt synchronously. We'd follow them and then accept the followbacks." — [transcript](https://github.com/ChatPRD/lennys-podcast-transcripts/blob/main/episodes/nikita-bier/transcript.md) [primary]
- Synchronous "moment": secondary write-ups add that the school Instagram accounts were **private** and that all follow requests were accepted at once **around 3:00 pm, when school let out**. Bier does not state the 3 pm timing in the transcript. — [DannyKPolitics Substack](https://dannykpolitics.substack.com/p/part-5-the-methods-of-nikita-bier); [rubenlicera.org](https://rubenlicera.org/28655/nikita-biers-remarkable-app-launch/) [secondary/blog, unverified]. Separately, Bier describes watching installs light up "around 4:00 PM and school had just gotten out" in Los Angeles, which confirms that after-school hours are the install peak — [transcript](https://github.com/ChatPRD/lennys-podcast-transcripts/blob/main/episodes/nikita-bier/transcript.md)
- Bier's explicit caveat: "This is not the way we grew the app. This is how we tested apps… people think we just went from school to school following every kid on it. You can't, that's totally unrealistic. But for the first 100 users, yes, that's how we got them." The goal was to remove the confounding variable of "did they add enough friends?" — "we would try to get an entire school to adopt, just to know if everyone had 10 friends, would they actually derive value." — [transcript](https://github.com/ChatPRD/lennys-podcast-transcripts/blob/main/episodes/nikita-bier/transcript.md) [primary]
- Rollout: "we were doing a state-by-state rollout strategy where every state was geo-fenced. We hadn't launched California until that morning." Engagement ran so hot (one school sent "450,000 messages in the first seven days"; about 60 messages per user on day one versus a typical 3–4) that they **geofenced/paused** to scale servers: "if it's working at this many individual schools, we could just relaunch it any time." — [transcript](https://github.com/ChatPRD/lennys-podcast-transcripts/blob/main/episodes/nikita-bier/transcript.md) [founder claim]
- Ops support: 24/7 in-app live chat support, run by one community rep (Michael Gutierrez), used as a real-time user research channel during rollouts. Peak acquisition was "360,000 per day." The team was 4 people (2 engineers, 1 designer, Bier), and Facebook acquired it about 9 weeks after launch. — [transcript](https://github.com/ChatPRD/lennys-podcast-transcripts/blob/main/episodes/nikita-bier/transcript.md) [founder claim]
- In-product loop: in 2017, tbh invites were sent server-side via Twilio SMS when a user tapped "Invite" on a contact. Messages were also shared to Snapchat. — [transcript](https://github.com/ChatPRD/lennys-podcast-transcripts/blob/main/episodes/nikita-bier/transcript.md)
- Content: all polls were written by the team so they were always positive ("who has the best smile, who's most likely to be president"). This was a deliberate anti-bullying design, after seeing negative messages on Sarahah. — [transcript](https://github.com/ChatPRD/lennys-podcast-transcripts/blob/main/episodes/nikita-bier/transcript.md)

**Gas (Aug 2022) — Nikita Bier**
- "We launched at the exact same school that we launched tbh on the exact same day five years later." It did not grow at first. Server-side SMS invites were no longer allowed ("It has to be sent from the user's device"), so invites dropped. It took "about… nine launches including renaming the app" to rebuild the growth loops. The staged validation questions were: do people send messages, then "will it spread within a school?", then "Will it hop schools?" — [transcript](https://github.com/ChatPRD/lennys-podcast-transcripts/blob/main/episodes/nikita-bier/transcript.md) [primary; founder claim]
- Rename: the app indexed 60–65% female, so they made the icon black with a flame and named it "Gas," and "the invites rate jumped." — [transcript](https://github.com/ChatPRD/lennys-podcast-transcripts/blob/main/episodes/nikita-bier/transcript.md)
- Onboarding used location to select your high school and add classmates. — [Analytics India Mag](https://analyticsindiamag.com/ai-news-updates/former-meta-exec-starts-hottest-social-media-app-ever-raises-suspicion) [press/secondary]. Gas was acquired by Discord about 5 months later — [Wikipedia: Gas (app)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gas_(app)).
- When a rumour hit, they "relaunch[ed] it on the other side of the country" under a different name. — [transcript](https://github.com/ChatPRD/lennys-podcast-transcripts/blob/main/episodes/nikita-bier/transcript.md)

**Yik Yak (Furman, Nov 2013) — Tyler Droll & Brooks Buffington**
- Launched on Furman's campus during **Homecoming weekend, November 2013**, a high-density social weekend. — [Furman News](https://www.furman.edu/news/furman-grads-take-yik-yak-to-new-level/) [press]. The Daily Tar Heel says it launched at **Furman and Wofford College** together, then spread across the Southeast in spring 2014 — [Daily Tar Heel](https://dailytarheel.com/article/2014/09/yik-yak-apps-herd-expands-at-unc).
- Seed channel: the founders emailed **student clubs, fraternities and sororities** at Furman and nearby colleges. Early targeting was specifically Greek life at Southeastern schools. It spread from Furman to Georgia Tech, then UGA. — [Wikipedia: Yik Yak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yik_Yak); [Business Insider India](https://businessinsider.in/How-2-Georgia-fraternity-brothers-created-Yik-Yak-a-controversial-app-that-became-a-400-million-business-in-365-days/articleshow/46544078.cms) [press]
- Penetration claim: "Within a week or two, a large majority of the campus was using it." — Buffington, [Upstate Business Journal, July 18, 2014](https://issuu.com/cjdesigns/docs/07182014ubj/12) [founder claim]
- Cross-campus jump: spring break. Early users shared it at Daytona Beach and Panama City, and "we just exploded." — [Business Insider India](https://businessinsider.in/How-2-Georgia-fraternity-brothers-created-Yik-Yak-a-controversial-app-that-became-a-400-million-business-in-365-days/articleshow/46544078.cms) [press; founder quote]
- The product itself is hyper-local (a feed of posts within a radius), so each campus is its own network by design. A formal "Campus Rep / Herder" program existed only in the 2021 relaunch (announced Dec 23, 2021). — [Wikipedia: Yik Yak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yik_Yak)

**Facebook (Harvard, Feb 2004 → college-by-college)**
- Launched **Wednesday, February 4, 2004** (afternoon) as a Harvard-only directory needing a harvard.edu email. Over 650 students had registered within about 4 days, and Zuckerberg predicted 900 by the next morning (Crimson, Feb 9, 2004, "Hundreds Register for New Facebook Website"). — [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/on-february-8th-2004-facebook-had-a-whopping-650-users); [Crimson 10-year feature](https://thecrimson.com/article/2014/2/4/facebook-ten-years-feature-1) [press]. A widely repeated "1,200 in 24 hours via Kirkland House email" claim could **not** be verified.
- Expansion order: Columbia (Wed, Feb 25, 2004), Stanford the next day, Yale on Mar 1, 2004. Zuckerberg said each school "took about three hours … to set up," and next targeted Boston-area schools (BU, MIT). Columbia already had a rival campus network (CU Community). — [Harvard Crimson, Mar 1, 2004](https://www.thecrimson.com/article/2004/3/1/facebook-expands-beyond-harvard-harvard-students/?print=1) [press]. Cornell followed around Mar 9 — [Cornell Sun](https://cornellsun.com/2004/03/09/facebook-connects-c-u).
- About a month in, it had reached "carefully chosen" colleges (Columbia, Stanford, Yale) and 10,000 sign-ups. — [Harvard Magazine](https://www.harvardmagazine.com/harvard-facebook-history-mark-zuckerberg-winklevoss) [secondary framing]

**Snapchat (Southern California high schools, 2012)**
- Spread started at an Orange County high school after Spiegel's mother told his cousin, a student there, about the app. Other SoCal high schools followed, and DAU went from about 3,000 to about 30,000 in a month in early 2012. — [Business Insider India](https://businessinsider.in/one-high-school-in-california-is-responsible-for-a-mobile-craze-3-4-million-people-use/amp_articleshow/21367883.cms) [press]
- Mechanism: the school had blocked Facebook on school-issued iPads, but Snapchat was too new to be blocked, so it was used in class. — [Harvard D3 student submission](https://d3.harvard.edu/platform-digit/submission/snapchat-at-school) [secondary, student-written]. Growth was word-of-mouth with no planned launch. — [TechCrunch, May 2012](https://techcrunch.com/2012/05/12/snapchat-not-sexting/)
- No source confirmed a "Venice High" origin. Treat that framing as unverified.

**BeReal (campus ambassadors, 2021–22)**
- Ambassadors recruited via Instagram DMs as early as June 2021. One Brown student was offered **$30 per referred signup**, and a group of 10 students later got $50 each via Venmo/PayPal. — [Brown Daily Herald, Feb 2022](https://www.browndailyherald.com/article/2022/02/new-social-media-app-tells-students-to-bereal) [press; single student account]
- Ambassadors were paid to promote at sponsored events and parties and to identify campus events "prime for BeReal involvement." BeReal declined to comment. Apptopia reported active users up 315% since the start of 2022. — [BestColleges, Apr 2022](https://bestcolleges.com/news/2022/04/22/social-network-bereal-college-students/); [Inside Higher Ed, Apr 8, 2022](https://www.insidehighered.com/news/2022/04/08/new-app-encouraging-students-be-real-sweeps-campus) [press]

**Fizz (Stanford, 2021)**
- Founded by Stanford freshmen Teddy Solomon and Ashton Cofer, who met in a freshman group chat. They first launched as "Buzz" on campus around August 2021 and renamed it Fizz in early 2022. — [Stanford Daily, Jan 23, 2022](https://stanforddaily.com/2022/01/23/from-buzz-to-fizz-anonymous-social-platform-takes-off-at-stanford/) [snippet]
- Tactic: free **donuts in exchange for downloads**, and bucket hats for posting about the app (seen at Dartmouth). Paid student ambassadors handed out flyers. — [The Dartmouth, Jul 2022](https://www.thedartmouth.com/article/2022/07/rojas-new-anonymous-app-fizz-launches); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=2408331) [press]
- Penetration claim: "used by 95% of Stanford undergraduates." — [Benzinga](https://drupal-cloudfront.benzinga.com/news/22/10/29148950/this-is-fizz-the-latest-social-media-dynamite-used-by-95-of-stanford-undergraduates) [founder claim]. TechCrunch flagged that adoption figures "might be a bit inflated." Fizz had 13 campuses at its Oct 2022 seed round and 25 under two months later. — [Stanford Daily, Oct 2022](https://stanforddaily.com/2022/10/05/stanford-startup-fizz-secures-4-5-million-in-seed-funding-targets-nationwide-expansion/) [snippet]; about 240 colleges and 60 high schools by mid-2024 — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2024/07/20/mark-zuckerberg-facebook-marketplace-empire-gen-z-fizz-college/)

**Lapse (2023 relaunch)**
- Hard invite gate: the app "only works with friends." Pre-installed members and creative-industry users were onboarded first, and each member could invite up to 5 friends. It reached #1 on the US App Store in Sept 2023. The co-founder claimed 100% organic growth. Users criticised it as pyramid-like. — [Lapse on Medium](https://medium.com/@lapse/introducing-lapse-the-shared-disposable-camera-for-friends-not-followers-b845d362f3ac); [PetaPixel, Sept 29, 2023](https://petapixel.com/2023/09/29/disposable-camera-app-lapse-tops-charts-by-making-users-invite-friends) [press + founder claim]
- Campus spread came through friend invite links (Northwestern, Villanova anecdotes), with no documented single-campus launch. — [Daily Northwestern](https://dailynorthwestern.com/2023/10/30/campus/lapse-or-bereal-nu-students-share-thoughts-on-photo-sharing-apps/)

**Sendit / Wizz**
- Sendit grew by piggy-backing on Snapchat (then Instagram) stories, not school by school. It gained 3.5M installs after Snap banned YOLO and LMK in 2021. Its 2022 Instagram version got 117K installs in 2 days. — [TechCrunch, Aug 3, 2021](https://techcrunch.com/2021/08/03/anonymous-snapchat-app-sendit-surges-with-3-5m-installs-after-snap-bans-yolo-and-lmk); [TechCrunch, Jun 29, 2022](https://techcrunch.com/2022/06/29/anonymous-social-apps-shift-their-attention-to-instagram-in-the-wake-of-snapchats-ban)
- Wizz (owned by Voodoo, launched first in France, US soft launch Jan 2020) is a stranger-discovery app with no school-by-school mechanics. It had 1.5M DAU in Sept 2023. — [Wikipedia: Wizz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wizz_(app))

### Inferences
- Common sequence: (1) choose **one dense, socially bounded unit** (a single school, a Homecoming weekend, the first week of term); (2) make the app visibly **about that school** (school-named IG account, .edu gate, school picker, school-targeted ads); (3) aim for **everyone at once** through simultaneous follow-back acceptance or a single event, so new users find friends already there; (4) watch the first 24h; (5) let organic spread to neighbouring schools prove the "school hop"; (6) open the next region with a geofence switch.
- After-school hours (about 3–4 pm local) and the first school week are the repeatedly cited timing windows. Specific weekday choice is not documented by any primary source.
- Bier's lesson matters for small teams: manual school saturation is a **product test** that delivers about 100 dense users. If the in-app invite loop doesn't then carry it, repeating the trick at more schools fails ("We've just done it at 15 schools and it's not working anymore").

### Gaps
- No primary source gives tbh/Gas exact launch weekday, or the number of IG accounts and follows.
- Fizz's ambassador pay and launch-day steps are undocumented (Stanford Daily full text unavailable — host blocked).
- No verified "Venice High" Snapchat origin, no first-week Yik Yak download counts, and nothing beyond product facts for Wizz and Sendit school growth.

## Q2. Saturn: how it spread school by school, ambassadors, and how it handled minors and school rules

### Takeaway
Saturn started as a schedule app Dylan Diamond built for his own school, Staples High (CT). It spread by standing up per-school apps run with **unpaid student ambassadors** who taught the team that school's bell schedule and kept it updated (snow days, delays). At first it white-labelled a separate app per school (17 before consolidating). It later replaced ambassador-maintained schedules with user consensus. A 2025 New York AG settlement exposed the minors/compliance weak points: no school-email or age verification, cross-school interaction, and undisclosed ambassador incentives.

### Cited Findings
- Origin: Diamond built a web app for Staples High as a junior because students were "obsessed with finding out who was in their classes at the beginning of the year." The founding date is cited as 2015 (first iteration) and 2018 (Saturn app) in different sources. — [Lenny's Newsletter guest post by Baron & Diamond, Dec 2023](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/lessons-on-building-a-viral-consumer) [snippet]; [Forbes, Aug 10, 2021](https://www.forbes.com/sites/alexandrasternlicht/2021/08/10/social-calendar-platform-saturn-raises-44-million-from-benioff-bezos-and-von-tobel-among-others/)
- Second school: "half of a second school joined in three hours," then it grew quickly to **17 schools, each with its own app**, before consolidating into one app. The company says it white-labelled an app for Weston High School (CT) and used that as its default model for most of its first 1,000 schools. — [Lenny's Newsletter](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/lessons-on-building-a-viral-consumer) [snippet; founder claim]
- Per-school onboarding cost: the team spent "a month working with a student ambassador to understand a school's schedule complexities." Later they decided to "entrust users with keeping schedules current instead of relying on ambassadors" via **consensus modelling**. — [Lenny's Newsletter](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/lessons-on-building-a-viral-consumer) [snippet]
- Ambassador role (Staples student paper, Feb 14, 2022): ambassadors explain the school's schedule and classes to the team at the start of each year and handle changes like snow days and delayed openings. The role is **unpaid**, with perks of early feature access, Saturn merch, and Saturn-funded social events the ambassador schedules through the app. — [Inklings News](https://www.inklingsnews.com/b/2022/02/14/students-collaborate-with-saturn-to-ensure-the-apps-success/) [press, student]
- Positioning: unpaid student ambassadors make the app feel "by and for students, rather than administrators." — via search summary of [SmartSocial](https://www.smartsocial.com/post/saturn) / [Medium](https://thruss09.medium.com/saturn-a-trending-app-with-familiar-cybersecurity-concerns-da1a0e1f58f3) [secondary]
- Competitive gamification: ambassadors earned **points** in a rewards system and "compet[ed]" against other schools for the highest number of Saturn users. Before August 2023 these promotions did not disclose the material connection. — [NY AG Assurance of Discontinuance, 2025](https://ag.ny.gov/sites/default/files/settlements-agreements/state-of-new-york-v-saturn-technologies-assurance-of-discontinuance-2025.pdf) [primary legal doc, via snippet]
- NY AG settlement (announced **Mar 7, 2025**, $650K total, of which $200K is payable): Saturn "failed to verify users' school email and age to ensure they were high school students and allowed users from different high schools to interact." Remedies: FTC endorsement training and disclosure for staff and ambassadors; limiting what users can see about non-Saturn students (class enrollment, events); hiding under-18 users' personal info until they consent to new terms; letting teachers block their name/initials from class schedules; deleting retained phone contacts. — [NY AG press release](https://ag.ny.gov/press-release/2025/attorney-general-james-announces-settlement-app-developer-failing-protect-young); [Hunton](https://hunton.com/privacy-and-information-security-law/ny-attorney-general-reaches-650-000-settlement-with-student-social-networking-app-developer-over-privacy-violations); [Kelley Drye](https://www.kelleydrye.com/viewpoints/blogs/ad-law-access/new-york-ag-settles-with-school-calendar-app-saturn)
- Scale: "nearly 20,000 schools" (founder write-up) and $44M raised in 2021 from Benioff, Bezos and others. — [Lenny's Newsletter](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/lessons-on-building-a-viral-consumer) [snippet]; [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/alexandrasternlicht/2021/08/10/social-calendar-platform-saturn-raises-44-million-from-benioff-bezos-and-von-tobel-among-others/). Bier says he advised Saturn and "rebuilt their Friend Finder." — [transcript](https://github.com/ChatPRD/lennys-podcast-transcripts/blob/main/episodes/nikita-bier/transcript.md)

### Inferences
- For a schedule-based app, the per-school cold-start cost is **data setup** (the bell schedule), not users. Saturn's path was ambassador hand-entry, then white-label per school, then crowdsourced consensus. A small team can shortcut this by letting the first student at a school enter the schedule and having others confirm it.
- The killer first-week hook is "see who is in your classes / when your friends are free," which is naturally strongest in the **first days of the school year** (see the Staples origin).
- Compliance lessons to copy from day one: verify school affiliation (school email or a classmate vouch), keep each school a closed network, disclose ambassador incentives, minimise visibility of non-users (e.g. teachers), and don't retain contact uploads.

### Gaps
- The full Lenny's Saturn post was not readable (host blocked), so the exact ambassador recruitment steps, launch timing per school and the "won school" metric are unconfirmed. How Saturn dealt with school administrations directly (as opposed to the regulator) is undocumented.

## Q3. Metric thresholds used to call a school "won" before moving on

### Takeaway
No founder published a formal "% of student body before expanding" rule. The numbers in public are anecdotal penetration benchmarks: tbh at 40% of a school in 24h, Saturn at half a second school in 3h, Yik Yak at "a large majority" in 1–2 weeks, Fizz at 95% of Stanford undergrads. Bier's practical gates were qualitative and staged: messages sent per user, then spread within a school, then spread to other schools.

### Cited Findings
- tbh: 40% of the seed school in the first 24h; 60 messages per user on day one (vs 3–4 typical); 450K messages in 7 days at one school. Later: "40% of the high school down your street in Los Angeles has downloaded it one week later." — [transcript](https://github.com/ChatPRD/lennys-podcast-transcripts/blob/main/episodes/nikita-bier/transcript.md) [founder claim]
- Gas stage gates: messages sent, then "will it spread within a school?", then "Will it hop schools?" (each "a very, very challenging problem"). — [transcript](https://github.com/ChatPRD/lennys-podcast-transcripts/blob/main/episodes/nikita-bier/transcript.md)
- Density target: "if everyone had 10 friends, would they actually derive value." — [transcript](https://github.com/ChatPRD/lennys-podcast-transcripts/blob/main/episodes/nikita-bier/transcript.md)
- Measurement tooling: a real-time installs map dashboard built by CTO Erik Hazzard, zoomable to a city block. — [transcript](https://github.com/ChatPRD/lennys-podcast-transcripts/blob/main/episodes/nikita-bier/transcript.md)
- Saturn: half of the second school in 3 hours [snippet, founder claim] — [Lenny's Newsletter](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/lessons-on-building-a-viral-consumer). Yik Yak: "large majority … within a week or two" — [UBJ](https://issuu.com/cjdesigns/docs/07182014ubj/12). Fizz: 95% of Stanford undergrads — [Benzinga](https://drupal-cloudfront.benzinga.com/news/22/10/29148950/this-is-fizz-the-latest-social-media-dynamite-used-by-95-of-stanford-undergraduates) (TechCrunch cautions it may be inflated). Facebook: 650+ of Harvard in about 4 days — [TNW](https://thenextweb.com/news/on-february-8th-2004-facebook-had-a-whopping-650-users).
- A secondary blog frames the tbh target as "40% penetration in 24 hours, then expand school by school" — [Synergy Labs](https://www.synergylabs.co/blog/how-nikita-bier-built-two-viral-apps-without-spending-a-dollar-on-marketing) [secondary/blog; turns an outcome into a rule].

### Inferences
- A defensible working threshold for a small team, built from these benchmarks: about 30–40% of one grade or school installing within 24–72h, plus evidence of **organic** installs at a neighbouring school that you did not seed. Treat the second signal as the real "won" test, since that is what Bier gated on.

### Gaps
- No source gives DAU/registered or retention thresholds per school, and none states an explicit "move-on" rule.

## Q4. Handling administrations, bans and bullying concerns

### Takeaway
Yik Yak responded to high-school threats by **geofencing about 85–90% of US middle and high schools** using a purchased school-boundary dataset (Maponics), with manual additions on request. tbh and Gas designed bullying out of the product (pre-written positive polls only). Gas also fought a viral "human trafficking" hoax by calling superintendents and police chiefs to retract it, steering press headlines, and getting Apple to remove review-bomb reviews. Saturn's issue was regulatory (NY AG). Sendit faced FTC action.

### Cited Findings
- Yik Yak (2014): after a bomb threat at San Clemente High School and lockdowns or evacuations in Marblehead MA and Decatur AL, the National Association of People Against Bullying asked it to disable the app at schools. Yik Yak geofenced middle and high schools via Maponics GPS boundaries. The co-founder said it was unavailable at 85% of them, and the company later said 90%, with more added on request. Its age policy was 17+. ### Inferences
- Pushback costs the most for anonymous and stranger-contact mechanics. A friends-only, school-closed, non-anonymous schedule app avoids most of these triggers but inherits Saturn's privacy obligations around minors and non-users.
- Prepare a rumour/hoax response kit before launch (parent FAQ page, a direct contact for administrators, fast App Store review reporting).

### Gaps
- No documented example of a founder negotiating proactively with a school administration before launch.

## Q5. What small teams copied cheaply

### Takeaway
The cheap, repeatable components are: school-named social accounts that follow students who list the school in their bio; school-targeted ads; a school picker or .edu gate so the app feels like "your school's"; invite caps or gates; giveaways (donuts, merch) and cash-per-referral student ambassadors; launching on a high-density day; and geofenced, region-by-region unlocking. Bier warns that copying the outreach trick without a working in-app invite loop fails.

### Cited Findings
- School IG account plus follow-back, and school-targeted ads, both used for the first ~100 users. Copycats report it "not working anymore" at 15 schools because it isn't the growth engine. — [transcript](https://github.com/ChatPRD/lennys-podcast-transcripts/blob/main/episodes/nikita-bier/transcript.md)
- "If you're targeting a community… you need to build marketing that shows imagery of that community… And then when you get in the app, you have to be able to join that community." — [transcript](https://github.com/ChatPRD/lennys-podcast-transcripts/blob/main/episodes/nikita-bier/transcript.md)
- Per-school setup is cheap: Facebook needed about 3 hours per school ([Crimson](https://www.thecrimson.com/article/2004/3/1/facebook-expands-beyond-harvard-harvard-students/?print=1)); Saturn used white-label per-school apps ([Lenny's](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/lessons-on-building-a-viral-consumer) [snippet]).
- Giveaways and ambassadors: Fizz used donuts for downloads and hats for posts ([The Dartmouth](https://www.thedartmouth.com/article/2022/07/rojas-new-anonymous-app-fizz-launches)); BeReal paid $30 per referral ([Brown Daily Herald](https://www.browndailyherald.com/article/2022/02/new-social-media-app-tells-students-to-bereal)); Saturn used unpaid ambassadors with merch and an inter-school points leaderboard ([Inklings](https://www.inklingsnews.com/b/2022/02/14/students-collaborate-with-saturn-to-ensure-the-apps-success/); [NY AG](https://ag.ny.gov/sites/default/files/settlements-agreements/state-of-new-york-v-saturn-technologies-assurance-of-discontinuance-2025.pdf)). The FTC disclosure requirement applies to all of these.
- Invite gate and caps: Lapse limited each member to 5 invites ([Medium](https://medium.com/@lapse/introducing-lapse-the-shared-disposable-camera-for-friends-not-followers-b845d362f3ac)).
- Emails to Greek life and clubs (Yik Yak) — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yik_Yak).
- Geofenced, state-by-state unlocking (tbh) — [transcript](https://github.com/ChatPRD/lennys-podcast-transcripts/blob/main/episodes/nikita-bier/transcript.md).

### Inferences
- A fake-door, school-specific waitlist with an "X% of your school has joined" counter is often attributed to these apps, but **no primary source here documents tbh, Gas or Saturn using a waitlist counter**. Treat it as a plausible pattern, not a documented one.

### Gaps
- No verified source on school-specific landing pages or waitlist counters for any of the named apps. Bier's X threads were not accessed.
